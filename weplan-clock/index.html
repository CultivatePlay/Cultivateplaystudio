        About co-creating

        A plan we make together

        WePlan Clock is a shared map of the session. The child helps decide what goes on it, sees how long each part lasts, and can ask to change it. The goal is predictability and a voice in the plan, so the child knows what is coming and trusts that they have a say.

        Predictability, not compliance

        Knowing what comes next, and when, can make a session feel safer. Many children find transitions easier when they can see the change coming. A visual schedule supports that.

        The schedule is not a tool to make a child obey. The clock is never the reason a child “has to” do something, and it isn't a reward chart or a countdown to a consequence. When a child says no to an activity, that tells us something about what they need, and the plan can change.

        Use it to
        • Show what's coming and how long it lasts
        • Give the child real choices
        • Talk about time together
        • Make changes visible when the child asks
        Avoid using it to
        • Insist “the clock says so”
        • Take away preferred activities as a consequence
        • Hurry a child who is regulating or engaged
        • Lock in a plan the child no longer agrees with

        Building the plan together

        • Offer choices. Let the child pick activities, or choose from a few options you set out.
        • Let them set the order. Dragging the cards around is a planning skill in itself.
        • Use their pictures. Photos of the real swing, the real obstacle course, or the child's own drawings mean more than generic symbols. Many children like taking the photos themselves.
        • Let them pick colors. Ownership grows from small choices.
        • Be open about your ideas. If there's something you'd like to try, share it as an invitation and let the child decide whether and where it fits.
        • Follow their interests. Build activities around what the child loves, like dinosaurs, trains or a favorite show, so the plan feels like theirs.

        “I'd love for us to try the climbing wall today. Where should it go in our plan?”

        “You picked swing and the obstacle course. Which one should come first?”

        Talking about time

        Time is hard to see, and that makes it hard to manage. Each wedge turns time into something the child can see shrinking as the minute hand moves through it. That supports time awareness, estimating and planning, which are all parts of executive functioning.

        • Estimate first. Ask how long the child thinks an activity will take before setting the minutes.
        • Notice time passing. Point to where the minute hand is: “We're in the green part. That's swing time.”
        • Compare. “We planned 10 minutes. Did that feel long or short?” There is no wrong answer; every child experiences time differently.
        • Use Split time evenly as a starting point, then talk about which activities need more or less.
        • Hand it over gradually. Over time the child can set the minutes, check the clock and say when it's time to move on.

        “How many minutes do you think the obstacle course needs?”

        “The minute hand is almost at the end of the orange wedge. What's coming next?”

        Check in often, and change the plan when asked

        A plan made together stays together. Check in during the session, especially near the end of each wedge. When the child asks for more time, less time, or something different, adjust the plan wherever it's safe and possible, and let them see the change on the clock. That shows their words make a difference.

        Use the 💬 Check in button during a session. It gives the child picture choices: more time, all done, change the plan, or it's going great. ✎ Change plan lets you edit together while the clock keeps running.

        Changing the plan isn't a failure. It's practice with flexible thinking, which is an important part of planning. When something truly can't change, explain why, and offer a choice inside it.

        “How's this going? Do you want more time, less time, or something different?”

        “Plans can change. Let's move it and see what the clock looks like now.”

        “Your ride is coming at 4:00, so swing can't go longer today. Do you want one more big push, or a slow countdown to stop?”

        Planning the day

        The My day option works the same way over a longer stretch. It uses the hour hand, so the whole clock face is 12 hours and each wedge might be an hour or more. Families can plan a weekend, a school break or a busy afternoon together, with the same choices, check-ins and changes.

        My week lets you choose a saved plan for each day, so the week is predictable too. On busy mornings, today's plan is ready to start.

        Using WePlan Clock

        1. Choose the activities together and add a picture for each.
        2. Estimate the minutes, or tap Split time evenly and adjust from there.
        3. Drag the cards into the order you agree on.
        4. Tap Start now, or pick the time the session started.
        5. Check in as you go and change the plan when the child asks.
        6. At the end, talk about how the time felt and save the plan for next time.
